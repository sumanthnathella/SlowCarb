# Vegetables & greens

Build a satisfying plate around vegetables **plus a substantial pulse or other protein serving**. Vegetables alone, a small spoon of dal, or watery rasam do not reliably make a protein-rich meal. See [pulses & protein](pulses-protein.md) and [staples & extras](staples-extras.md).

!!! info "What these categories mean"
    **Fits** means a useful choice for this site's interpretation of Ferriss-style slow-carb eating. **Adaptation** means a practical variation, not explicit author approval. **Leave out** means omit during the six strict days if following the author's framework—not that the food is unhealthy. Ferriss does not classify every Indian ingredient: most vegetable-versus-starch mappings below are our interpretations. See [sources and scope](../sources.md).

Local names vary by region and can describe more than one plant. Confirm the actual ingredient rather than relying on a transliteration alone.

## Gourds and everyday vegetables

These fit the non-starchy vegetable side of the plate when cooked without flour coatings, potatoes, sugar, or dairy. They are not interchangeable with pulses as protein sources.

## Edible pods versus shelled beans

| Ingredient | Names and distinction | Category |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Yardlong / long beans | Karamani pods, alasandala pods; chauri, chawli, lobia pods | Fits as a vegetable; mature cowpeas are pulses. |
| Green / French beans | Beans; fansi, farasbi | Fits as a vegetable, not a protein anchor. |
| Cluster beans | Kothavarangai; goruchikkudu; guvar, guar, gawar | Fits; cook until tender. |
| Flat beans | Avarakkai; chikkudukaya; surti papdi, val papdi | Tender edible pods fit as vegetables. Remove strings and cook thoroughly. |
| Shelled papdi / field beans | Papdi lilva, val, avarai beans | Count the shelled seeds as legumes, not “free vegetables”; see [protein guide](pulses-protein.md). |

## Greens, stems and brassicas

All entries here fit our vegetable mapping. Rotate varieties rather than treating one green as a supplement or cure.

| Ingredient | Common names / preparation |
| --- | --- |
| Spinach | Palak, pasalai keerai, palakura; wash and cook or use safely washed tender leaves. |
| Amaranth greens | Thotakura, mulai/arai keerai varieties, chaulai, tandaljo; names cover several varieties. |
| Fenugreek leaves | Methi, vendhaya keerai, menthikura; fresh or plain dried leaves. |
| Moringa leaves | Murungai keerai, munagaku; strip tough stems. |
| Sorrel / roselle leaves | Gongura, pulicha keerai, ambadi; sour greens, not a medicinal requirement. |
| Malabar spinach | Basella, kodi pasalai, bachali kura, poi saag; cook as greens. |
| Mustard, radish and beet greens | Sarson saag; mooli leaves; beetroot leaves; wash grit thoroughly. Beet greens are distinct from the root below. |
| Banana stem and flower | Vazhaithandu / arati doota; vazhaipoo / arati puvvu, kele ka phool; trim and cook. These are **not banana fruit**. |
| Cabbage, cauliflower, broccoli | Muttaikose/patta gobi; cauliflower/phool gobi; broccoli. Avoid flour batter and cheese sauces. |
| Radish, turnip, kohlrabi | Mullangi/mooli; shalgam; noolkol/ganth gobi. These are not automatically excluded merely because they grow near or below ground. |

## Vegetables that need a distinction

- **Carrots:** fit; Ferriss explicitly mentions carrots. Do not invent a blanket ban on all root vegetables.
- **Green peas:** fit the book's vegetable list, but a small serving is not a protein anchor. Mature dried peas belong in the pulse category.
- **Beetroot and orange pumpkin:** use moderate portions as a **practical adaptation** if desired. Varieties, serving sizes, and starch content vary; this is not an explicit Ferriss classification. Pair with pulses and other vegetables.
- **Leave out on six strict days:** potato; sweet potato/shakarkand/sakkaravalli kizhangu; elephant-foot yam/suran/senai kizhangu/kanda; taro/arvi/arbi/seppankizhangu/chamadumpa; purple yam/ratalu; cassava/tapioca/maravalli kizhangu; raw banana/vazhakkai/aratikaya; corn/maize; breadfruit; and green jackfruit. This is our conservative starch mapping, not a claim that the author named each one. Names such as “kanda” need regional confirmation.

## Fresh, frozen or canned?

Plain frozen vegetables follow the **same ingredient categories as fresh**. Frozen spinach fits; frozen corn remains in the strict-day leave-out category. Check mixed bags for corn, potato, sauce, butter, flour, or sugar. Canned vegetables can also work when their ingredients fit; rinse if useful for reducing salt.

Ingredient eligibility and gluten cross-contact are separate questions. Read the full package, allergen statement, and gluten-free labeling appropriate to your needs; a vegetable name on the front does not certify the product. No local store stock is implied here.
