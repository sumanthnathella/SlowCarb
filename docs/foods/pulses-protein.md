# Pulses & vegetarian protein

Keep the default plate dairy-free: a generous pulse serving, plenty of [vegetables](vegetables.md), and an additional protein source when needed. A thin bowl of rasam or sambar may contain very little dal; its name and volume do not establish its protein content.

!!! info "Framework, not a prescription"
    Ferriss's targets of roughly **20 g protein per meal and 30 g at breakfast** are author rules, not personalized medical requirements. Needs vary with body size, activity, age and health. People with kidney disease or a prescribed diet should use individualized advice. Indian pulse mappings below are interpretations; a nutrient table does not prove an ingredient is explicitly author-approved. See [sources](../sources.md).

## Pulse reference

Amounts are approximate grams of protein per **one cooked cup of drained beans or thick cooked pulses**, not one cup dry. Variety, water content and cup size change the result; use a package or recipe calculation when precision matters. These fit this site's legume interpretation of slow-carb eating.

| Pulse | Searchable names | Approximate protein |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pigeon peas | Toor, tur, arhar dal; thuvaram paruppu, kandi pappu | 11–15 g |
| Mung beans, whole or split | Moong; pachai payaru, pasi paruppu; pesalu, pesara pappu | 14–16 g |
| Red/brown lentils | Masoor, masoor dal | 16–18 g |
| Black gram | Urad; ulundhu; minumulu, minapa pappu | 12–16 g |
| Bengal gram, split or whole | Chana dal, kala chana; kadalai paruppu, kondakadalai; senaga pappu | 13–15 g |
| Chickpeas | Kabuli chana, chole, white kondakadalai | 14–15 g |
| Cowpeas / black-eyed peas | Lobia, chawli; karamani; alasandalu | 12–14 g |
| Kidney beans | Rajma | 13–15 g |
| Horse gram | Kollu; ulavalu; kulthi | About 12–16 g |
| Moth beans | Matki, moth | About 13–15 g |
| Mature field / hyacinth beans | Val, avarai beans, anapa ginjalu; shelled papdi lilva | About 10–14 g; fresh seeds vary |
| Black, pinto and navy beans | Black beans are not urad; pinto beans; navy/haricot beans | 14–16 g |
| Mature soybeans | Soyabean; not the same portion value as green edamame | About 28–31 g |
| Mature dried peas | Dried green/yellow peas, split peas, vatana, pattani | 15–17 g |

Pulse-rich kootu, thick dal, sundal and bean curries are useful foundations. For example, a cup of chickpeas gives around 14–15 g—not automatically 20–30 g. Increase or complement the serving as appropriate rather than counting a garnish as the main protein. There is **no need to combine specific proteins at every meal**; adequate total intake and variety across the day matter. Rice is not required to “complete” every dal meal.

## Other protein choices

| Choice | How to classify it here | Practical protein guide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eggs | Included in the starter plan's fixed breakfast; not required at every meal | Around 6–7 g per large whole egg; 2 whole eggs + 2 whites provide roughly 20 g before vegetables and toppings. |
| Egg whites | An option for adjusting the omelette's protein without adding another whole egg | Around 3.6 g per large white; use the carton label for liquid whites. |
| Plain tofu | Optional if tolerated, not a starter-plan default; practical vegetarian adaptation, not an approval inferred from a nutrient table | Often 10–20 g per 100 g firm tofu; check the brand. |
| Plain tempeh | Practical adaptation; avoid barley/grain-containing versions and unsafe gluten cross-contact | Often 18–20 g per 100 g. |
| Soy chunks / textured vegetable protein | A departure from a conservative minimally processed reading, not inherently unhealthy | Often 12–15 g per 25 g **dry**; hydrated weight is misleading. Check additives and gluten labeling. |
| Unsweetened pea, hemp or rice protein powder | Options discussed by the book; optional convenience, not a requirement or permission for rice as a staple | Commonly 15–25 g per labeled serving; hemp products can be lower. Choose gluten-free, dairy-free products without added sugar. |
| Lactose-free cottage cheese | Optional adaptation only; keep the default dairy-free | Often 10–13 g per 100 g. Not interchangeable with paneer; check ingredients and actual protein. |
| Crispy roasted edamame (e.g., Biena) | Packaged legume snack or meal topper; practical soy adaptation, not a protein anchor for a meal | About 13 g per labeled 30 g serving (roughly 130 kcal). Measure a serving into a bowl — a full bag is several servings. Plain salted flavors list only edamame, oil, and seasoning and are labeled gluten- and dairy-free; check other flavors for dairy, wheat-based seasonings, or sugar. |

The [fixed daily breakfast](../meals/recipes.md#fixed-breakfast-avocado-and-spinach-omelette) is half an avocado topped with 1 tbsp hemp hearts, 1 tsp ground flax, 1 tsp pine nuts, and gluten-free bagel seasoning, alongside a spinach omelette with 2 whole eggs + 2 whites. Using 3 whites instead of 2 can bring the total closer to the author's roughly 30 g breakfast recommendation once the avocado, spinach, and toppings are included; it does not guarantee 30 g. Portions, egg size, and product labels change the estimate.

Lactose-free dairy still contains milk proteins and is unsuitable for milk allergy. Ordinary paneer is not reliably lactose-free. Nuts, avocado and coconut can add flavor or energy but are **not dependable main protein portions**.

## Cook safely and make pulses comfortable

Rinse and sort dried pulses. Soak larger beans as directed, discard soaking water, add fresh water and cook fully; split dals often need no soaking. Pressure-cooker times depend on the bean, age, altitude and equipment. Use a tested method rather than assuming one whistle suits everything.

!!! warning "Kidney beans need proper boiling"
    Raw or undercooked kidney beans can cause poisoning. For dried kidney beans, follow reliable food-safety guidance: soak at least five hours, discard that water, then boil vigorously in fresh water for at least 30 minutes before further simmering. Do not cook raw dried kidney beans only in a slow cooker. Commercially canned kidney beans are already cooked.

Use cultivated food varieties of field beans and cook thoroughly; do not eat unknown or raw mature beans. Cook sprouts thoroughly, especially for people at higher risk of foodborne illness.

For gas or bloating, start with smaller portions, increase gradually, rinse canned beans and try well-cooked split mung. Soaking may help tolerance but does not guarantee symptom-free meals. Persistent symptoms deserve assessment, not progressively harsher restrictions. Refrigerate cooked pulses promptly and reheat safely.
