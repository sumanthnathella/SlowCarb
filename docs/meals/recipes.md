# Five practical recipes

These are **recipe adaptations**, not claims of exact Ferriss approval. All default to egg-free, gluten-free, and dairy-free. Use oil, not ghee; choose gluten-free spices and hing or omit hing. Small amounts of lemon or tamarind are practical culinary exceptions. See [principles](../principles.md) and [sources](../sources.md).

Protein estimates are rough, per serving, and depend on ingredient weights and labels, especially tofu. They are planning aids, not prescribed targets. Dry pulse quantities below are measured **before soaking**.

## Mung-only pesarattu with tofu bhurji

**Serves 2.** Makes about four medium pancakes, depending on thickness.

- ½ cup dry whole mung beans, approximately 100 g
- 1 cm ginger, ½ teaspoon cumin, optional green chili
- Approximately ⅓–½ cup water for blending, more as needed
- 2 teaspoons oil for the pan; salt to taste
- One full batch of tofu bhurji below
- Optional: ½ small avocado, divided between plates

1. Soak mung in plenty of water for 8–12 hours in the refrigerator; drain and rinse.
2. Blend with ginger, cumin, chili, salt, and enough water for a pourable but not watery batter. No rice or rice flour is needed.
3. Lightly oil a medium-hot skillet. Spread a quarter of the batter thinly. Cook until the top loses its wet sheen and the underside browns; flip and cook through. Repeat, adjusting heat so the center cooks before the outside burns.
4. Divide pancakes and bhurji between two plates. Add avocado if wanted.

**Approximate protein:** often around 25–40 g per complete meal, depending on tofu. Pancakes alone provide roughly 10–15 g per serving. Increase tofu if your label shows a lower-protein product and you want a more substantial meal.

## Tofu bhurji

**Serves 2.** Use with pesarattu or as the protein component of a curry bowl.

- 300 g drained firm tofu, crumbled
- 100 g onion, chopped
- 200 g tomato, chopped
- 100 g spinach, chopped
- 2 teaspoons oil
- ½ teaspoon cumin seeds, ¼ teaspoon turmeric
- Chili, black pepper, and salt to taste
- Optional: 1 teaspoon lemon juice for the whole batch

1. Heat oil; add cumin, then onion, and cook until softened.
2. Add tomato, turmeric, and chili. Cook until the tomato breaks down.
3. Add tofu and spinach. Stir and cook until everything is hot and the spinach has wilted. Adjust seasoning; finish with optional lemon.

**Approximate protein:** about 15–25 g per serving with many firm tofus; check your package. If using eggs, cook them until set and recalculate the portion rather than assuming a one-for-one replacement.

## Ridge gourd moong pappu

**Serves 3.** Pair each serving with approximately 150 g tofu and a vegetable base.

- 1 cup dry split yellow moong dal, approximately 200 g, rinsed
- 600 g ridge gourd, peeled and chopped
- 200 g tomato and 100 g onion, chopped
- 3 cups water initially; more as needed
- 2 teaspoons oil, ½ teaspoon mustard seeds, ½ teaspoon cumin
- ¼ teaspoon turmeric, curry leaves, salt, and chili to taste

1. Taste a small piece of ridge gourd before cooking; discard the whole vegetable if unusually bitter.
2. Bring dal, water, and turmeric to a boil, then simmer partly covered until beginning to soften. Add water if needed to prevent sticking.
3. Add gourd and tomato. Simmer until dal is fully soft and vegetables are tender; mash lightly. Cooking time varies with the dal and pan.
4. In another pan, heat oil and crackle mustard and cumin. Add onion, curry leaves, and chili; cook until onion softens. Stir into the dal and season.

**Approximate protein:** about 15–20 g per serving before tofu. A thin ladle of pappu is less than one-third of this batch.

## Green bean paruppu usili

**Serves 3.** Pair with tofu; this is not a deep-fried preparation.

- ¾ cup dry chana dal, approximately 150 g
- 600 g green beans, trimmed and chopped
- 1 teaspoon cumin, optional dried chili
- 1 tablespoon oil, ½ teaspoon mustard seeds
- Curry leaves, pinch of turmeric, salt to taste

1. Soak chana dal in the refrigerator for 6–8 hours. Drain; coarsely grind with cumin, chili, salt, and just enough water to move the blades.
2. Spread in a shallow greased steamer dish. Steam until fully cooked and firm throughout, usually around 20 minutes depending on thickness. Check the center; extend cooking if still raw or gritty. Cool slightly and crumble.
3. Steam or simmer beans until tender; drain excess water.
4. Heat oil, crackle mustard, and add curry leaves and turmeric. Add pulse crumbles and beans; stir until hot, breaking up clumps. No rice flour is needed.

**Approximate protein:** roughly 10–15 g per serving. For a complete bowl, add about 200 g firm tofu per person, warmed in a skillet with spices and a splash of water.

## Adapted avial without yogurt

**Serves 3 as a side, not a protein dish.**

- 200 g ash gourd, 150 g snake gourd, and 150 g long beans
- 1 drumstick pod, approximately 100 g, cut into lengths
- 1 small carrot, approximately 75 g
- 3 tablespoons grated unsweetened coconut
- ½ teaspoon cumin, optional green chili
- ¼ teaspoon turmeric, 1 teaspoon oil, curry leaves, salt
- 1 teaspoon unsweetened tamarind pulp diluted in ¼ cup water; use less if concentrated

1. Cut vegetables into similar-sized pieces. Simmer drumstick, beans, and carrot with turmeric and enough water to prevent sticking. Add gourds and cook until all are tender.
2. Grind coconut, cumin, and chili with a little water. Stir into the vegetables with diluted tamarind and simmer briefly.
3. Finish with oil and curry leaves; adjust salt. Eat the soft drumstick interior and discard its fibrous shell.

No yam, taro, raw banana, potato, or yogurt. Protein is low, only a few grams per side serving; pair with dal and tofu rather than counting avial as the meal’s protein.

!!! warning "Cooking and storage"
    Cook pulses thoroughly; soaking alone is not cooking. Refrigerate cooked food in shallow containers within two hours, or one hour above 32°C/90°F. Use refrigerated leftovers within 3–4 days and freeze extras promptly; do not keep a whole week’s meals in the refrigerator. Reheat leftovers thoroughly to 74°C/165°F. Follow package instructions for tofu storage and thaw frozen portions in the refrigerator.
